InVideo vs Canva

Compared 2026-10-17 using published specs and documentation.

Canva is the everything-design tool; InVideo is video-first with a separate prompt-to-video AI product. The right pick depends on whether video is your main output or one of many.

TL;DR: Winner for dedicated video work: InVideo. Its Studio editor is built around video timelines and 6,000+ video templates, and AI Plus ($17/mo) gives access to top video models (Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0) that Canva doesn't match. Canva wins if you need one tool for all design, with video as a side task.

The credit question

InVideo AI runs on credits: Plus gives 750/mo (≈300 Nano Banana Pro image generations by InVideo's own example), and unused credits don't roll over. Canva bundles AI inside the subscription with softer limits. If prompt-to-video is your core workflow, InVideo's model shelf (Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0) is the differentiator; if AI is occasional, Canva's bundle is simpler.

Who should pick which

Marketers cutting regular social/promo videos: InVideo Studio. Creators testing AI video generation cheaply: InVideo AI Plus — the cheapest credible entry to top video models. Generalists who design everything else too: Canva Pro.

Our verdict

Our verdict: InVideo for video-first creators (especially AI generation at $17/mo). Canva for one-tool-does-everything teams where video is occasional.

Is InVideo better than Canva for video?

For dedicated video editing and AI video generation (Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0 access), yes. Canva is broader but shallower on video.

How much does InVideo cost vs Canva?

InVideo Studio from $15/mo and AI Plus $17/mo (annual); Canva Pro ~$12.99/mo. InVideo's AI runs on credits that don't roll over.
