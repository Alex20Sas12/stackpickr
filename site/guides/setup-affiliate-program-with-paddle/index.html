How to set up an affiliate program with Paddle (2026)

Updated 2026-10-08.

If you sell software through Paddle and want affiliates, the first thing to know: Paddle's job is payments and tax compliance (merchant of record), not partner marketing. There is no built-in affiliate dashboard with links, cookies and payouts. Teams running affiliates on Paddle pair it with a dedicated tracking platform. This guide covers the realistic setups.

1. What Paddle does and does not give you

Paddle handles checkout, subscriptions, global sales tax (VAT/GST), invoicing and chargebacks as the merchant of record. What it does not include: unique affiliate links, cookie-based attribution, affiliate sign-up portals, commission calculation and automated payouts. Those come from an affiliate tracker connected to Paddle.

2. How attribution works with Paddle

The standard pattern: the tracker generates each affiliate's links, drops a cookie (or first-party redirect), and listens for a signal that a sale happened. With Paddle that signal comes from Paddle webhooks (subscription activated / payment succeeded events) or from periodic reconciliation against Paddle's reporting API. When a tracked conversion event matches a cookie, the tracker credits the affiliate. If a platform advertises a native Paddle integration, it means this webhook wiring is pre-built; if not, expect custom middleware.

3. Your options, compared

Tapfiliate — lists a Paddle integration, SaaS pricing, white-label affiliate area; see our Tapfiliate review and Tapfiliate vs Rewardful.
Post Affiliate Pro — long-standing Paddle integration, self-hosted option, one-time-ish pricing tiers.
PartnerStack — B2B SaaS partner programs (referral + reseller), heavier contracts.
Spreadsheet + manual — for tiny programs: Paddle reporting CSV matched against affiliate links you issue yourself. Works under ~20 affiliates, breaks above.

4. Setup checklist

  • Pick a tracker that documents Paddle support (webhook or API reconciliation).
  • Configure Paddle webhook notifications (subscription_activated, payment_succeeded) pointing at the tracker.
  • Decide commission model: flat % (typical 20–30% for SaaS) or recurring (Paddle subscriptions make recurring commissions natural — check the tracker bills affiliates monthly against Paddle's renewal events).
  • Set cookie window (30/60/90 days) and payout schedule.
  • Test end-to-end with a $1 sandbox subscription before inviting affiliates.
  • Write affiliate terms — Paddle is merchant of record, so refunds and chargebacks flow back to commissions; the tracker must claw those back automatically.

5. When Paddle is the wrong base for affiliates

If your program will be mostly B2C coupon/deal traffic, merchant-of-record platforms add friction (no direct customer relationship). Paddle shines for global B2B/SaaS where tax compliance outweighs everything. For a broader view of tracker choice independent of the billing stack, see our affiliate software comparisons.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Paddle have a native affiliate program feature?

No. Paddle is a merchant of record for billing and tax — affiliate links, attribution, dashboards and payouts come from a separate tracker (e.g. Tapfiliate or Post Affiliate Pro) connected via Paddle webhooks or its reporting API.

How does an affiliate tracker know a Paddle sale happened?

Via Paddle webhook events (subscription activated / payment succeeded) or by reconciling against Paddle's reporting API. Trackers with a documented Paddle integration have this wiring pre-built.

What commission rate is typical for SaaS on Paddle?

20–30% recurring is the common band for B2B SaaS. Because Paddle handles subscriptions, recurring commissions renew automatically each billing cycle — verify your tracker claws back commissions on refunds and chargebacks, which Paddle passes through.

Can I run a small Paddle affiliate program with just a spreadsheet?

Under roughly 20 affiliates, yes: issue tracked links yourself and match Paddle's reporting CSV monthly. Above that, cookie attribution and payout math break down — that is the point where a dedicated tracker pays for itself.
