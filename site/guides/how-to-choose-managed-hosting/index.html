How to choose managed hosting in 2026

Updated 2026-10-14.

Managed hosting is supposed to remove server work. Use this checklist to verify a provider actually delivers on that promise.

1. Backups included, not add-ons

Automated daily backups should be standard. If backups cost per-GB extra (DigitalOcean snapshots, AWS snapshots), price that into the real monthly cost.

2. Support that fixes hosting problems

Test the difference: 'we'll answer your ticket' vs 'our engineers will fix your server'. Cloudways support operates on the second model, 24/7, with phone support on request.

3. Staging environments

One-click staging prevents production outages. It's included on Cloudways; on raw hosts you build it yourself.

4. Scaling without migration

Vertical scaling (more CPU/RAM on the same box) should be a click. Cloudways scales servers live; plan-based hosts force tier jumps.

5. Security handled for you

Managed patching, firewall, free SSL auto-renewal. Self-managed boxes need all of this configured by you.

6. No artificial caps

Visit limits and install limits (common on WordPress-only hosts) are pricing levers disguised as features. Prefer hosts limited only by your server size.

7. Try before you commit

Cloudways gives a 3-day trial without a credit card — the low-risk way to verify all six points above on your own site.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should managed hosting cost?

Quality managed hosting starts around $11/mo (Cloudways entry). WordPress-only premium hosts typically start $20-35+/mo.

Do I need managed hosting for WordPress?

If you don't want to manage updates, caching, backups and security yourself — yes.

Can I switch providers later?

Yes — choose hosts with standard stacks (Linux + PHP + MySQL) and free migration assistance to keep exits open.
